GLIMPSE

JWST's GLIMPSE: gravitational lensing & NIRCam imaging to probe early galaxy formation and sources of reionization

Ultra-deep JWST imaging of the lensing cluster Abell S1063 to find the faintest galaxies in the early universe.

ESA/Webb, NASA & CSA, H. Atek, M. Zamani (ESA/Webb); Acknowledgement: R. Endsley

At a glance

120 h science time
9 NIRCam filters
30.9 AB 5-sigma depth (0.2 arcsec aperture)
>33 AB intrinsic (de-lensed) depth
~4.4 arcmin2 source-plane area at z~6
~540 galaxy candidates at 6 < z < 16

The survey

GLIMPSE measures the abundance of faint galaxies at z > 6 down to M_UV ~ -12, setting the first observational benchmarks for galaxy formation and stellar feedback models at these luminosities.

The survey constrains how much the faintest galaxies contribute to cosmic reionization, the process that turned the intergalactic medium from neutral to ionized.

It also probes the typical galaxy population during the Dark Ages at z > 10, rather than just the record-breaking sources found so far.

We will detect galaxies at z>6 more than a thousand times fainter than the Milky Way.

About GLIMPSE

GLIMPSE is JWST GO program 3293, an ultra-deep NIRCam imaging survey of the lensing cluster Abell S1063. Co-PIs: Hakim Atek (CNRS / Institut d'Astrophysique de Paris) and John Chisholm (University of Texas at Austin).
