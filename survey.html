Survey

GLIMPSE is JWST GO program 3293, "JWST's GLIMPSE: gravitational lensing & NIRCam imaging to probe early galaxy formation and sources of reionization." This page summarizes the program, the target, the observing strategy, and the science goals.

Target

The survey targets Abell S1063 (also known as AS1063, ACO S 1063, or RXC J2248.7-4431), a Hubble Frontier Fields cluster at redshift z = 0.348. The NIRCam pointing is centered on the proposal target ACO-S-1063, at RA 22 48 52.19, Dec -44 31 52.1.

Abell S1063 was chosen for its regular morphology, a compact high-magnification region that fits within a single NIRCam module, and the best-constrained lens model among the Frontier Fields clusters, built on more than 70 spectroscopic multiple images from VLT/MUSE.

The survey covers an effective source-plane area of about 4.4 arcmin2 at z~6. That is a lensing-corrected area behind the cluster, not the area of sky observed.

Observations

GLIMPSE observes Abell S1063 in nine NIRCam filters, seven broadband and two medium-band, with wavelength coverage from 0.8 to 5 microns. Readout used the DEEP8 pattern with a 4-point primary plus 4-point subpixel INTRAMODULEBOX dither.

Science goals

  1. Measure the abundance of faint galaxies at z>6 down to M_UV ~ -12, establishing the first observational benchmarks for galaxy-formation and stellar-feedback models in this regime.
  2. Constrain the contribution of the faintest galaxies to cosmic reionization.
  3. Probe the typical, not just record-breaking, galaxy population during the Dark Ages at z>10.
We will detect galaxies at z>6 more than a thousand times fainter than the Milky Way.

Ancillary data

  • HST imaging from the Hubble Frontier Fields program, and HST FLASHLIGHTS (PID 15936)
  • VLT/MUSE spectroscopy, more than 70 multiple images with spectroscopic redshifts
  • JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution multi-object spectroscopy from the GLIMPSE-D / DDT program
  • Archival Chandra X-ray, ALMA, and VLA radio coverage of the cluster

About GLIMPSE

GLIMPSE is JWST GO program 3293, an ultra-deep NIRCam imaging survey of the lensing cluster Abell S1063. Co-PIs: Hakim Atek (Institut d'Astrophysique de Paris) and John Chisholm (University of Texas at Austin).
